Why MP3 transcoding loses quality: bitrate and repeated encoding

MP3 trades some audio information for smaller files and broad player support. Conversion cannot invent detail missing from the source.

Lossy encoding discards information

MP3 is a lossy audio codec. It uses perceptual coding to reduce the information needed to represent sound. Information discarded during encoding cannot be fully recovered by converting the result to WAV or FLAC. Similar perceived sound does not imply identical data. Audible differences depend on the material, encoder, bitrate, listening conditions and playback equipment.

Three different operations

OperationWhat changes
FLAC or PCM WAV to MP3A lossy playback copy is created. A lossless source does not make the resulting MP3 lossless.
Lossy OGG or AAC to MP3Audio is decoded and encoded again using a lossy codec. This can add further loss. A higher output bitrate cannot restore information lost previously.
Supported NCM or QMC recoveryThe tool attempts to recover the audio inside its wrapper and preserves the original encoding. This is not an automatic MP3 transcoding step.

What 192 kbps means here

Ordinary audio conversion uses 192 kbps MP3. At this bitrate, one minute is approximately 1.44 MB and five minutes approximately 7.2 MB, excluding metadata and other overhead. Bitrate describes data rate, not an objective score for a recording, master or source file.

Existing MP3 files are kept as-is after validation to avoid unnecessary re-encoding. Supported special formats retain their original audio encoding. Ordinary conversion depends on browser decoding support. This tool does not offer adjustable bitrates or a lossless MP3 option.

Reduce avoidable loss

  1. Keep the best available original and create a separate MP3 playback copy.
  2. Convert from the original rather than making chains such as MP3 to AAC to MP3.
  3. Preview quiet passages, dense instruments and high-frequency material, then test in your actual player.
  4. If your player already supports the original format, conversion may be unnecessary.

Common misconceptions

Does converting MP3 to FLAC make it a lossless source?

No. FLAC can store the decoded sound losslessly, but it cannot restore information already discarded by MP3. The file may simply become larger.

Does a higher output bitrate always sound better?

No. More storage or a higher bitrate cannot create missing source information. It also does not establish the quality of the original recording or mastering.
